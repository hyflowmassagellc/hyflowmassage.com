Couples & Gifts

Couples Massage at Home:
A Bay Area Holiday Gift Guide

By Tina Zhang, CMT  ·  November 2026  ·  6 min read

Every December, people ask me the same question: “What do you give a couple who already has everything?” My answer is usually time — two quiet hours together, without traffic, parking, a babysitter or a drive home afterward. That is exactly what couples massage at home is designed for.

This guide explains how an in-home couples session works, how to plan a relaxing evening around it, a few honest safety notes, and what it costs in the Bay Area. Whether you are planning a gift for your partner, your parents or friends, the idea is the same: we come to you.

The Short Answer

With couples massage at home, two licensed therapists bring two tables to your living room and massage you side by side at the same time — each person with their own style and pressure. It costs $600 for 90 minutes or $800 for 120 minutes for two people, travel included within about 37 miles of Mountain View (+$50 / +$80 per therapist per visit in extended areas). It is about relaxation and time together — not a medical treatment.

How Couples Massage at Home Works

A Couples Massage at Home session is simple for you and carefully organized on our side:

  • Two therapists, two tables. We arrive with professional tables, fresh linens, bolsters and oils, and set up in about 10–15 minutes — not taken from your session time.
  • Side by side, at the same time. You and your partner are massaged together in the same room, so neither of you is waiting.
  • Different massages are fine. One of you can choose a relaxing Swedish massage while the other prefers deep tissue or wellness lymphatic drainage — each at your own pressure.
  • Your choice of therapists. Sessions are performed by Tina Zhang or California-licensed therapists on her team; female or male therapists are available on request.

All you need is a room of about 10 × 12 ft — a living room or a large bedroom works well — and a few minutes to move a coffee table.

Why It Makes a Good Gift

The U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) notes that massage may help with short-term relief of anxiety and pain, and the Mayo Clinic describes massage as one way people reduce stress and tension and promote relaxation, while noting that more research is needed. Those are modest, realistic benefits — and they are exactly what most busy couples are missing in December.

What makes the at-home version different is everything around the massage. There is no rushing to a spa after work, no driving home relaxed and then sitting in traffic on 101, and no need to book a babysitter if the kids are asleep upstairs. When the session ends, you are already home.

Five Ideas for Planning the Evening

Before
Pick a calm time. Many couples choose an evening after the kids are in bed, or a weekend afternoon. We are open daily 9 AM – 9 PM.
Setting
Keep it simple. Dim lights, a comfortable room temperature and quiet music are plenty. Silence phones — that is half the gift.
Choice
Let each person choose. Talk about pressure and focus areas beforehand — shoulders and neck after long desk days, legs after holiday travel.
After
Plan nothing. Drink some water, have a light dinner and enjoy a slow evening while we quietly pack up.
Extra
Make it a ritual. For a special occasion, the VIP Wellness Ritual at Home for couples adds gentle Tai Chi, lymphatic drainage and neck & shoulder release.

A Few Honest Safety Notes

For most healthy adults, massage from a trained, licensed therapist is low-risk. According to the Mayo Clinic, people should talk with their doctor first if they have a bleeding disorder or take blood-thinning medicine, have had a recent blood clot, or have burns, open wounds, a recent fracture or severe osteoporosis. When you book, please let us know about:

  • Pregnancy: prenatal massage is often possible with side-lying positioning, but check with your doctor or midwife first, especially in a high-risk pregnancy.
  • Recent surgery: wait for your surgeon's clearance. For post-surgical swelling, a Post-Op Lymphatic Drainage session is the better fit than a relaxation massage.
  • New or one-sided leg swelling, pain or warmth: this can be a sign of a blood clot and needs a doctor, not a massage.
  • Medications and health conditions: we adapt every session and will ask you to check with your doctor if anything is unclear.
