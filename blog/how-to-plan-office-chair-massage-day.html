How to Plan an Office Chair Massage Day:
A Simple Guide for HR and Office Managers

By Tina Zhang, CMT  ·  November 2026  ·  7 min read

An on-site chair massage day is one of the easiest wellness events to run. There is no venue to book, no catering to order and nothing for employees to prepare — they step away from their desks for 15 minutes and come back a little looser and calmer. But a few small decisions make the difference between a day that feels rushed and one your team talks about for weeks.

Here is the checklist we share with HR teams and office managers across the Bay Area, from the first email to the follow-up.

The Short Answer

Pick a date and a goal, then plan about four employees per therapist hour at 15 minutes each. Book 1–2 weeks ahead (earlier for December and January), reserve a quiet corner about 6 × 8 ft near an outlet, and share a sign-up sheet a few days before. At HyFlow, a 4-hour visit is $600 and covers about 14–16 employees; you can book online or request a quote.

Step 1 · Choose the Occasion and the Date

Chair massage works best when it is tied to a moment your team will notice:

  • Holiday or employee appreciation events in November and December
  • A New Year wellness week in January, when many teams set healthier goals
  • Busy season or deadline weeks — quarter-end, product launches, tax season for accounting firms
  • Offsites, conferences and health fairs, where a massage station draws people in

Mid-week days (Tuesday to Thursday) and late morning to mid-afternoon usually have the best turnout. Avoid days packed with all-hands meetings.

Step 2 · Decide How Long and How Many Therapists

The math is simple: one therapist sees about four employees per hour at 15 minutes each, or about two per hour at 30 minutes. Allow a little time for setup and short breaks.

For a bigger team, add therapists rather than hours — two therapists for six hours ($1,800) can see up to about 48 employees. If you are unsure how many people will sign up, start with one therapist and open a waitlist — it tells you exactly how much to book next time.

Step 3 · Prepare the Space

You do not need a spa room. A small, quiet space works well:

  • A corner or small meeting room about 6 × 8 ft per therapist
  • A power outlet nearby if possible, and a chair outside for the next person
  • Away from busy walkways, kitchens and loud phone areas
  • Parking or loading-zone details and a building contact for check-in

We bring the professional massage chairs, face-cradle covers and sanitizing supplies, and clean the chair between every employee.

Step 4 · Announce It and Open Sign-Ups

Share a sign-up sheet with 15- or 30-minute slots three to five days before the event — by email, Slack, Teams or a printed sheet in the break room. A short announcement is enough:

Sample Announcement

“Take a 15-minute break on us! Licensed massage therapists will be in the 2nd-floor small conference room on Thursday from 11 AM to 3 PM. Chair massage is done fully clothed — no oils, no changing. Grab a slot on the sign-up sheet. If you have a recent injury, are pregnant or have a medical condition, just let your therapist know.”

Keep a short waitlist. People who miss out tend to be the first to ask, “When are you bringing them back?”

Step 5 · On the Day

  • Your therapist arrives about 15 minutes early to set up.
  • Each employee answers a few quick health questions and chooses their pressure.
  • Sessions focus on the neck, shoulders, upper back, arms and hands — the areas desk work tightens most.
  • We keep the schedule on time so every slot gets its full session.

Step 6 · Follow Up

A few days after the event, send a two-question survey (“Did you enjoy it?” and “How often would you like this?”). Most teams use the results to decide between a monthly visit ($600–$2,100 per month) or a weekly visit ($2,600–$13,650 per month). See the full recurring price table.

Vendor Paperwork, Made Easy

If your company needs a vendor setup before the first visit, ask early. We provide a certificate of insurance (COI) on request, complete vendor setup forms, invoice your company directly and accept corporate card or ACH, with Net-30 terms available.

A Quick Safety Note

Chair massage is light and fully clothed, and serious problems are uncommon when it is done by a trained, licensed therapist. According to the Mayo Clinic, people who have a bleeding disorder, take blood-thinning medicine, have had a recent blood clot, a recent heart attack, broken bones or severe osteoporosis should check with their healthcare team before massage, and pregnancy also affects how massage is done. Our therapists ask every employee a few short questions first and adjust or skip areas when needed.
