Out-of-Network Insurance Reimbursement for Massage Therapy:
How Superbills Work, Step by Step

By Tina Zhang, CMT  ·  November 2026  ·  6 min read

"Does insurance cover massage?" is one of the questions we hear most — especially from clients recovering from surgery, managing chronic pain or going through cancer treatment who need a series of sessions. The answer is often "possibly, through out-of-network benefits." Here is how that works, in plain language.

The Short Answer

HyFlow Massage LLC is not in-network with insurance plans, so we do not bill your insurer directly. You pay at the time of service, and we give you a superbill on request — an itemised receipt you submit to your insurer for possible partial reimbursement. Whether you get money back depends on your plan. Call your insurer first, and ask your doctor for a prescription if your plan requires one.

What "Out-of-Network" Means

An in-network provider has a contract with your insurance company and bills them directly. An out-of-network provider does not. Many PPO and some POS plans still pay part of the cost of out-of-network care — but you pay the provider first, then ask your insurer to reimburse you. HMO and EPO plans usually do not cover out-of-network care except in emergencies.

What Is a Superbill?

A superbill is a detailed receipt with the information insurers need to process a claim. It typically includes:

  • Your name, date of birth and contact details
  • The provider's name, business details and credentials
  • Dates of service and the length of each session
  • Service (procedure) codes describing the treatment
  • Diagnosis codes — usually taken from your doctor's prescription or referral
  • Itemised charges and the amount you paid

Source: GoodRx — What Is a Superbill?

Questions to Ask Your Insurer Before You Start

Call the number on the back of your insurance card and ask:

  • Do I have out-of-network benefits, and is massage therapy a covered service?
  • Do I need a prescription or referral from my doctor, or prior authorisation?
  • What is my out-of-network deductible, and how much have I met this year?
  • What percentage do you reimburse after the deductible, and is there a limit on the number of visits?
  • Do you accept superbills? How do I submit them, and what is the deadline?

Write down the date, the name of the person you spoke with and a reference number for the call.

Step by Step: Getting Reimbursed

Step 1
Check your benefits with the questions above.
Step 2
Get a prescription from your doctor or surgeon if your plan requires one — for example, "manual lymphatic drainage after liposuction" or "therapeutic massage for low-back pain", with a diagnosis and suggested number of sessions.
Step 3
Book and pay for your sessions as usual — in our Mountain View studio or at home.
Step 4
Ask us for a superbill. Tell us when you book or text (408) 668-6562. We can prepare one per visit or one for a series.
Step 5
Submit the claim through your insurer's online portal, by mail or by fax, together with the superbill and your prescription if required.
Step 6
Watch for your Explanation of Benefits (EOB). If approved, the insurer usually pays you directly. If denied, the EOB gives the reason — you can correct and resubmit or appeal.

Also Use HSA and FSA

Even if your insurance does not reimburse massage, HSA or FSA funds can often be used when massage is for a specific medical condition, usually with a Letter of Medical Necessity from your doctor. If you have FSA dollars left, check your plan's year-end deadline. More in HSA & FSA for Massage Therapy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does insurance cover massage therapy?

Some plans do, usually through out-of-network benefits and often only with a doctor's prescription for a specific condition. HMO and EPO plans usually do not cover out-of-network care. Call your insurer to check your benefits before you start.

Is HyFlow Massage in-network with insurance?

No. HyFlow Massage LLC is out-of-network and does not bill insurers directly. You pay at the time of service, and we provide a superbill on request that you can submit to your insurer for possible reimbursement.

What is a superbill for massage?

A superbill is an itemised receipt that includes your details, the provider's details and credentials, dates of service, service and diagnosis codes, and the amount paid. You submit it to your insurer, often with your doctor's prescription, to request out-of-network reimbursement.

How do I submit a superbill to my insurance?

Most insurers accept superbills through their online member portal, by mail or by fax. Include your doctor's prescription if your plan requires one, keep copies, and check your Explanation of Benefits for the result. Reimbursement is not guaranteed.
