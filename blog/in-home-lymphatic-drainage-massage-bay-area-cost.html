In-Home Lymphatic Drainage Massage in the Bay Area:
Cost, What to Expect & How to Prepare

By Tina Zhang, CMT  ·  November 2026  ·  7 min read

Lymphatic drainage is one of the few massages that works even better at home. The strokes are slow and light, the goal is to leave your body calm and your fluid moving — and the last thing that helps is getting up from the table, putting on shoes and sitting in traffic on 101. That is why mobile manual lymphatic drainage (MLD) is now our most-booked service.

Here is what an in-home session costs with HyFlow Massage LLC, what actually happens when we arrive, and how to get your space ready.

What Happens During an In-Home Session

Before
Booking and intake. You book online or by text. We confirm your address, parking, pets and — for post-op clients — your surgery date and written surgeon clearance.
Arrival
10–15 minutes of setup. Your therapist brings a professional table, fresh linens, bolsters and unscented products. Setup time is not taken from your session.
Session
Light, rhythmic work. MLD feels very gentle — closer to a skin stretch than a deep massage. We begin at the neck and collarbone, clear the main lymph node areas, then work toward the swollen areas. Many people fall asleep.
After
Stay where you are. Drink water, put your compression garment back on if you wear one, and rest. We pack up quietly. Most clients notice they need the bathroom more over the next few hours.

How to Prepare Your Space

  • A clear area about 8 × 10 ft (2.5 × 3 m) — a living room, bedroom or hotel room works
  • A warm room; you will be lying still under a sheet
  • Parking instructions, gate or building codes
  • Pets settled in another room
  • For post-op sessions: your clearance letter, compression garment and any surgeon instructions

You can request a female or male therapist when you book. We speak English, Mandarin Chinese and Russian.

When Not to Book

Lymphatic drainage is not a substitute for medical care. Please check with your doctor first if you have active infection or fever, a suspected blood clot, heart or kidney failure, or are in active cancer treatment. Contact your doctor right away for sudden swelling or pain in one leg, spreading redness or heat, or chest pain or trouble breathing.
