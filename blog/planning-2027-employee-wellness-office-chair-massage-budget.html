Planning 2027 Employee Wellness:
How to Budget a Year of Office Chair Massage

By Tina Zhang, CMT  ·  November 2026  ·  7 min read

Late fall is when many HR teams, office managers and people-ops leads sit down with next year's budget. If on-site chair massage is on your list — or your team keeps asking for it after a holiday event — this guide will help you turn “we should do that more often” into a simple, predictable plan for 2027.

It covers how often to schedule visits, how many employees each visit covers, what a full year costs, the vendor paperwork your finance team will ask for, and a sample calendar. All prices are our current rates; nothing is hidden.

The Short Answer

Office chair massage is $150 per therapist hour, 4–7 hours per visit ($600–$1,050). One 4-hour visit covers about 14–16 employees at 15 minutes each. A year of monthly visits costs $7,200–$12,600, twice a month $14,400–$25,200, and weekly visits $31,200–$54,600. Most teams start with a single $600 pilot, then choose a rhythm. Travel is included within about 37 miles of Mountain View (+$50 / +$80 per therapist per visit in extended areas).

Why Teams Are Budgeting for It

In the American Psychological Association's 2023 Work in America survey, 77% of workers reported work-related stress in the past month, and 92% said it is important to work for an organization that values their emotional and psychological well-being. A recurring chair massage visit is one visible, easy-to-use way to show that care. The U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) notes that massage may help people feel less anxious and more relaxed in the short term — modest benefits, but real ones for a busy office.

Chair massage is also low-friction: employees stay fully clothed, there are no oils, and a 15-minute session fits between meetings. It is a wellness perk, not medical care, and it works best alongside your existing health benefits.

Step 2: Match Visit Length to Headcount

  • 15-minute sessions: about 14–16 employees in 4 hours, or 26–28 in 7 hours, per therapist.
  • 30-minute sessions: about 7–8 employees in 4 hours, or 13–14 in 7 hours.
  • Example: 20 employees at 15 minutes each take about 5.5 hours, roughly $825 per visit.
  • Larger teams: book 2 therapists ($1,200–$2,100 per visit) or 3 therapists ($1,800–$3,150) at the same time — see large events with 2–3 therapists.

A practical rule: plan for about 60–70% of the team to sign up at first. Participation usually grows once people try it.

Step 3: Start With a Pilot

If you are not sure which rhythm fits, book one 4-hour event ($600) in January or February, with no contract. Count sign-ups, collect a quick thumbs-up survey, and decide from real numbers. Many teams then move to once a month and add visits later. Our guide on how to plan an office chair massage day has a setup checklist and a sample sign-up announcement.

Step 4: Get Vendor Setup Done Early

Finance and procurement teams usually ask for the same things, so it helps to request them in January:

  • Certificate of insurance (COI) — provided on request, naming your company if needed.
  • Vendor setup forms — we complete them on request.
  • Invoices to your company — paid by corporate card or ACH, with Net-30 available.
  • Licensed therapists — sessions are performed by Tina Zhang or California-licensed therapists on her team; female or male therapists are available on request.

A Quick Note on Comfort and Safety

Chair massage is gentle and fully clothed, but employees who are pregnant, recently had surgery or an injury, take blood-thinning medicine, or have a health condition they are unsure about should check with their doctor first, and tell the therapist before the session. Our therapists adjust pressure and positioning for each person, and anyone can skip an area or stop at any time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a year of office chair massage cost in the Bay Area?

At $150 per therapist hour with 4–7 hour visits, a year of monthly visits costs $7,200–$12,600, twice a month $14,400–$25,200, and weekly visits $31,200–$54,600. Two or three visits a week cost $62,400–$163,800 per year. Travel is included within about 37 miles of Mountain View; East Bay and other extended areas add +$50 or +$80 per therapist per visit.

How many employees can one visit cover?

One therapist covers about 14–16 employees at 15 minutes each in a 4-hour visit, or about 26–28 employees in 7 hours. With 30-minute sessions, plan on half as many. For larger teams, book two or three therapists at the same time.

Should we start with monthly or weekly visits?

Most teams start with one 4-hour pilot event ($600), then move to once or twice a month. Weekly visits make sense once sign-up slots fill quickly and employees ask for more.

Can you provide a certificate of insurance and invoices?

Yes. We provide a certificate of insurance (COI) and complete vendor setup forms on request, invoice your company, and accept corporate card or ACH. Net-30 is available.

Do employees need to undress or change for chair massage?

No. Chair massage is done fully clothed, with no oils, in about 15–30 minutes, so employees can go straight back to work.
