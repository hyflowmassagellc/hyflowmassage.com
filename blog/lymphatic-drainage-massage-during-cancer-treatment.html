Gentle Care

Lymphatic Drainage & Gentle Massage During and After Cancer Treatment:
Benefits, Limits and Safety

By Tina Zhang, CMT  ·  November 2026  ·  7 min read

Cancer treatment asks a great deal of the body. Surgery, chemotherapy, radiation and hormone therapy can leave people exhausted, sore, anxious, swollen and sleeping badly — often for months. Many of our clients tell us the same thing: they are tired of being "a patient" and simply want to feel cared for in their own body again.

Gentle massage and light manual lymphatic drainage (MLD) can be part of that care. But it is important to be clear and honest about what they can and cannot do.

The Short Answer

Gentle massage and light lymphatic drainage are supportive comfort care. Research suggests massage can ease pain and help people relax during cancer care, and many clients feel calmer, sleep better and feel less heavy afterwards. They do not treat or cure cancer, and they do not replace your oncology team or a certified lymphedema therapist. Written clearance from your oncologist or physician always comes first.

What Gentle Touch Can Help With

  • Pain and discomfort. The 2022 Society for Integrative Oncology–ASCO guideline on integrative approaches to cancer pain states that massage may be offered, especially to people with pain during palliative or hospice care, based on a randomized trial in which massage brought clinically meaningful pain relief (ASCO Post summary).
  • Stress and anxiety. Slow, predictable touch and quiet time help many people move out of "fight or flight" — something that is hard to find between appointments and scans.
  • Sleep and fatigue. Many clients rest more deeply on the evening of a session. Treatment-related fatigue is complex, but feeling relaxed is a good start.
  • A feeling of heaviness or puffiness. Light, rhythmic lymphatic drainage strokes are very gentle and many people describe feeling lighter afterwards.
  • Reconnecting with your body. After surgery or months of procedures, kind and respectful touch can feel healing in a very human way.

What It Cannot Do

  • It does not treat, shrink or cure cancer, and it is not an alternative to any medical treatment.
  • It does not "detox" the body or "flush out" cancer cells. Gentle massage does not spread cancer, but it is also not a way to remove it.
  • It is not lymphedema treatment. Diagnosed lymphedema is managed by a certified lymphedema therapist (CLT), usually with complete decongestive therapy — specialised MLD, compression bandaging or garments, exercise and skin care. A Cochrane review found MLD is safe and well tolerated and may add a modest benefit to compression bandaging, but the evidence is limited (Cochrane review).

At HyFlow, Tina Zhang is trained in the Vodder method of manual lymphatic drainage through Klose Training. She is not a CLT, so if you have diagnosed lymphedema, we work alongside — never instead of — your lymphedema therapist and doctor.

Safety First: How We Adapt Every Session

  • Written clearance from your oncologist or physician before your first session
  • Very light pressure — no deep tissue work during active treatment
  • We avoid tumour sites, fresh incisions, drains, ports and PICC lines, and skin being treated with radiation
  • We pause sessions if you have a fever or infection, a suspected blood clot (pain, warmth or swelling in one leg or arm), very low blood counts, or new unexplained pain or swelling — please call your care team
  • After lymph node removal, we follow your team's guidance about the affected arm or leg
  • Positioning with pillows and bolsters so you are comfortable, including side-lying or semi-reclined
  • Unscented products, because smell can be sensitive during chemotherapy

Questions to Ask Your Oncology Team

  • Is gentle massage and light lymphatic drainage safe for me right now?
  • Are there areas of my body to avoid (surgery, radiation, ports, lymph node removal)?
  • Are my blood counts or clotting risk a concern this week?
  • Should I see a certified lymphedema therapist for any swelling?

Frequently Asked Questions

Can lymphatic drainage massage help during cancer treatment?

Gentle massage and light lymphatic drainage can be supportive comfort care during and after cancer treatment, with your oncologist's approval. A 2022 SIO–ASCO guideline says massage may be offered for cancer-related pain, especially in palliative or hospice care, and many people find it relaxing. It does not treat or cure cancer.

Does massage spread cancer?

Gentle massage does not spread cancer. Sessions are adapted for safety: very light pressure, and avoiding tumour sites, incisions, ports, radiation-treated skin and areas your care team asks you to avoid. Written clearance from your oncologist or physician is required.

Is lymphatic drainage the same as lymphedema treatment?

No. Diagnosed lymphedema is treated by a certified lymphedema therapist (CLT), usually with complete decongestive therapy that combines specialised MLD, compression, exercise and skin care. HyFlow's sessions are supportive comfort care and work alongside, not instead of, your lymphedema therapist.

Can I use out-of-network insurance for massage during cancer treatment?

HyFlow Massage LLC is out-of-network, but we provide a superbill on request that you can submit to your insurer. Some plans reimburse massage when it is medically recommended, and some require a prescription or referral from your doctor. HSA and FSA funds can often be used with a Letter of Medical Necessity. Reimbursement is not guaranteed, so check with your plan first.
